For examples, donkeys are really used (and quite useful in narrow souks). Olive trees are abundant. Half-completed construction is everywhere. We often saw school childen coming or going. Jardin Majorelle

When we arrived in Marrakesh, our first stop was the wonderful garden created by Jacques Majorelle and later restored by fashion designer Yves Saint-Laurent and Pierre Bergé. Upon entering the garden, we found a cool refuge from the hot Marrakesh air. Tall bamboo shaded extensive red terracotta walkways. The garden has winding paths that lead through different sections, for example, a water lily pond and a cactus garden. Also, inside we loved the Berber Museum which offered a compact, high quality collection of all sorts of arts and crafts from Berber life. Outside the garden, a mini fashion district has taken hold, with many nice shops and cafes. It was a great side trip just a short taxi ride from the Medina. ...

Julian at Davies

Julian Lage brought his new quartet featuring organist John Medeski, bassist Jorge Roeder and drummer Kenny Wollesen to Davies Symphony Hall on a Monday evening. I was excited to bring a group of colleagues from Digital Promise and we had a great time listening to adventuresome music in the grand space. Compared to other times I’ve heard Lage, he seemed more restrained–allowing more space for his very talented bandmates to shine. John Medeski’s work on organ and piano commanded attention and offered many different tones and textures. At one point, when he played nearly alone, the deep resonances of the organ in spacious Davies symphony hall were particularly magical. Although he allowed more space for others, Julian still had plenty of room to feature his amazing talents. ...
